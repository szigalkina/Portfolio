# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters, hiring managers, and senior product/tech leaders evaluating Svetlana Zigalkina de Boer for product management and creative-technology roles. They arrive cold (from a CV link or LinkedIn), skim first, and decide within a minute whether to read deeper. Secondary audience: peers and collaborators checking craft.

## Product Purpose

A single-page portfolio that tells Svetlana's story: product leadership (Shell, blockchain energy traceability, IoT wearables), an AI lab of hands-on experiments, six years of energy-domain depth, and formal credentials. It must prove two things simultaneously: (1) she ships real products with measurable outcomes, and (2) she has rare taste and command of AI-native tools (the site itself, built with Claude Code, is the demo). Success = a recruiter scrolls the whole page, remembers "the hotel portfolio", and books an interview.

## Brand Personality

Whimsical-precise-grand. The Grand Budapest Hotel as an interface: symmetrical compositions, jewel-box color, brass and velvet, concierge-level attention to detail. Wes Anderson's deadpan elegance, never kitsch, never a theme park. The visitor is a hotel guest being walked floor by floor through her career.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI-generated portfolio aesthetics: cream/beige defaults, purple gradients, identical card grids, tiny uppercase eyebrow labels over every section.
- Template portfolios (Squarespace/Webflow sameness, hero-metric blocks, stock-photo professionalism).
- Kitsch: literal movie-prop cosplay, novelty cursors, pastel overload that sacrifices readability.
- Corporate slide-deck flatness (the content must feel curated, not exported from PowerPoint).

## Design Principles

1. **The hotel is the information architecture.** Floors = chapters; the elevator doors, keys, and plaques are navigation, not decoration. Every metaphor must carry a real wayfinding job.
2. **Symmetry as confidence.** Centered, planimetric compositions with one deliberate asymmetric break per chapter for emphasis.
3. **Proof over adjectives.** Outcomes (€22M rNPV, 5+ assets, live in market) get typographic prominence; the design frames evidence like exhibits in a vitrine.
4. **Concierge detail.** Micro-interactions are small, impeccable, and in-world (a bell, a ledger line, a key tag), never generic bounce effects.
5. **The site is the skill demo.** Craft level itself communicates "built with Claude Code, directed by a person with taste."

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large display text ≥3:1, full keyboard navigability for interactive elements (accordion, gallery, fan-stack), `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for scroll-scrub and entrance choreography, alt text in the brand voice for all imagery.
